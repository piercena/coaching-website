About Nick

You might be here because your mind feels too crowded, your projects are sitting half-finished, and your instinct to plan everything out has quietly turned into a cage.

Maybe nothing is dramatically “wrong” on paper. You have a career, responsibilities, and capability. But you know deep down that you are spinning your wheels—caught in an endless loop of over-analysis, preparation, and hesitation.

You are not broken.

You’ve just been trained to believe that if you think hard enough and get every single detail right, you can avoid failure.

And I know that firsthand. Because for years, I lived inside that exact trap.

When Preparation Becomes a Hiding Place

For the longest time, my default response to uncertainty was to retreat into my head.

I would start a dozen different projects, map them out in infinite detail, and never actually ship a single one. Why? Because as long as it lived only in my head, I didn't have to risk exposure or embarrassment. At work, I’d have great visions for teams and architecture, but hesitation would creep in whenever it was time to execute.

I brought that exact paralysis into my relationships, too.

I could feel a conflict coming on so I would close my mouth and try to plan out my next move. Thinking through every possible terrible outcome for what I might say, I left my partners feeling like they were talking to a wall with no emotions or empathy.

That is what the over-analysis loop does. Every new thought becomes a distraction. Plates overflow, tasks pile up, and you slip straight into a private shame spiral—all because you confused thinking with doing.

Finding a Different Path

I didn't break out of that loop by reading another strategy book or designing a more complex plan.

I broke out of it when I joined a men’s group, stopped hiding, and learned the power of momentum. I realized that small, imperfect action wins over immaculate planning every single time. I learned how to stack small wins, take a hit without collapsing, and ask for help when I needed to course-correct.

In my marriage, I stopped searching for the "right" thing to say to avoid conflict. I learned to talk with total candor—building a space where connection matters more than comfort, and where the freedom to make mistakes keeps vitality alive.

"Perfectionism isn't high standards. It's a hiding place."

Real Authority & Radical Responsibility

Personal responsibility is brutal to learn and painfully easy to forget.

Over the last 15 years, I’ve had my heart broken in a failed engagement, walked away from misaligned career paths, and hit walls of intense systemic burnout. Every single time I cracked open those breakdowns, they pointed back to one root poison: victimhood—waiting for permission, waiting for certainty, or waiting for the stars to align.

I know what it looks like because I’ve lived in the wreckage of it.

Today, I draw on a real breadth of execution: I’ve helped launch a tech platform from scratch, watched it fail, and rebuilt my career to lead engineering teams. I’ve spent over half a decade building a marriage where conflict is celebrated, mistakes are honored, and connection feels secure. And every single week, I sit in a men's group holding space for men stepping out of their shadows and into their power.

I don’t teach touch-free self-help fluff. I teach the mechanical discipline of cutting the noise, dropping the victim mindset, and trusting yourself enough to take your next step.
